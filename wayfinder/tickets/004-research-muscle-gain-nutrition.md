## Question

What are the evidence-based nutrition targets for someone starting resistance training who wants to build muscle and be healthy?

Specifically:

1. Daily protein intake for muscle growth (g/kg), per meta-analyses and position stands (e.g. ISSN, Morton et al. 2018) — and whether distribution across meals matters.
2. Calorie surplus: how large should it be for a beginner to gain muscle without excessive fat, and how is it estimated and adjusted?
3. What else matters at the "be healthy" level — dietary pattern, fiber, whole foods vs supplements — per mainstream dietary guidance?
4. The sleep–nutrition–recovery interplay: what does evidence say about sleep deprivation's effect on muscle gain, appetite, and pain sensitivity?

Findings go to `wayfinder/research/004-muscle-gain-nutrition.md`, every claim cited to its primary source.

## Resolution

Findings in [`wayfinder/research/004-muscle-gain-nutrition.md`](../research/004-muscle-gain-nutrition.md), fully cited. Gist:

- **Protein**: ~1.6 g/kg/day is where the meta-analytic benefit plateaus (Morton 2018); 1.6–2.2 g/kg while gaining. Per-meal spread (~0.4 g/kg over 3–5 meals) is a minor refinement — the daily total dominates.
- **Surplus**: small — ~10–20% over maintenance (+200–400 kcal), targeting 0.25–0.5% body weight gain/week; bigger surpluses add fat, not extra muscle. Estimate maintenance (Mifflin-St Jeor), then calibrate off the weekly-averaged weight trend.
- **Health layer**: whole-food dietary pattern, fiber ~14 g/1,000 kcal, food-first. Only creatine (3–5 g/day) has strong supplemental evidence for muscle gain; vitamin D matters only if deficient.
- **Sleep is load-bearing**: sleep restriction cuts muscle protein synthesis ~18–19%, shifts weight change away from lean mass, disrupts appetite hormones (ghrelin up, leptin down), and measurably lowers pain thresholds. ≥7 h/night is the single highest-leverage recovery intervention — this directly feeds the sleep-and-habits ticket.
